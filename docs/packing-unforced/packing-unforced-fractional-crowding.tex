\subsection{Fractional moments and rich near-bisector incidences}
\label{crowding:fractional-section}

Retain the fixed positive kernel and the notation
\(\Pi_A,\sigma_y,\Lambda_A,H_\varepsilon=N_\varepsilon+F_\varepsilon\)
of Section~\ref{crowding:section}. The criterion below permits an
unbounded ordinary collision integral. It supplies regularity of the
full selected law, whereas Proposition~\ref{crowding:positive} requires
only a positive absolutely continuous component.

\begin{proposition}[Fractional far crowding]\label{crowding:fractional}
Fix \(0<\gamma<1\). If some sequence \(\varepsilon_j\to0\) satisfies
\[
 L:=\sup_j\int F_{\varepsilon_j}^{\gamma}\,d\Pi_A<\infty,
\]
then \(\Lambda_A\) has an \(L^{1+\gamma}(d\nu\,dt)\) density \(f\),
with \(\|f\|_{1+\gamma}^{1+\gamma}\le L\).
In particular, the same conclusion holds for every
\(0<\gamma<\min\{1,\beta\}\) if, uniformly along this sequence,
\begin{equation}\label{crowding:fartail}
 \Pi_A\{F_\varepsilon>t\}\le C_*t^{-\beta}
 \qquad(t\ge1)
\end{equation}
for some \(\beta>0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\varphi_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1}
\mathbf1_{[-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2]}\), and write
\(f_\varepsilon(y,t)=\varphi_\varepsilon*\sigma_y(t)\).
Tonelli gives
\[
 \int f_\varepsilon^{1+\gamma}\,d\nu\,dt
 =\int d\nu(y)\int d\sigma_y(r)\,
  \varepsilon^{-1}\int_{|t-r|\le\varepsilon/2}
                    f_\varepsilon(y,t)^\gamma\,dt.
\]
For every \(t\) in the inner interval,
\(f_\varepsilon(y,t)\le
\varepsilon^{-1}\sigma_y([r-\varepsilon,r+\varepsilon])\).
Pulling \(r\) back to \(|x-y|\), and using subadditivity, yields
\begin{align}
 \int f_\varepsilon^{1+\gamma}\,d\nu\,dt
 &\le\int H_\varepsilon^\gamma\,d\Pi_A\notag\\
 &\le\int F_\varepsilon^\gamma\,d\Pi_A
   +\Pi_A(\R^2\times\R^2)^{1-\gamma}
       \left(\int N_\varepsilon\,d\Pi_A\right)^\gamma.
 \label{crowding:fractionalnear}
\end{align}
The last term tends to zero by \eqref{crowding:near}. This application
of concavity is on the original fixed selected pair measure; it changes
neither selection factor. Weak compactness in \(L^{1+\gamma}\),
testing against continuous functions, and lower semicontinuity identify
the limit with \(\Lambda_A\) and give the asserted norm bound.
Finally, layer cake and \(\Pi_A(\R^2\times\R^2)\le1\) give
\[
 \int F_\varepsilon^\gamma\,d\Pi_A
 \le1+\frac{\gamma C_*}{\beta-\gamma}
 \qquad(\gamma<\beta),
\]
proving the tail implication.
\end{proof}

The fractional hypothesis is strictly weaker than uniform quadratic
collision control at the level of radial laws. For example, fix
\(0<\beta\le1\), set \(a=(1+\beta)^{-1}\), and consider the
probability \(d\sigma(t)=(1-a)t^{-a}\mathbf1_{(0,1)}dt\).
Its density belongs to every \(L^{1+\gamma}\) with \(\gamma<\beta\),
but not to \(L^2\). Moreover
\[
 \varepsilon^{-1}\sigma([r-\varepsilon,r+\varepsilon])
 \le C(r+\varepsilon)^{-a},\qquad
 \sigma\{H_\varepsilon>t\}\le Ct^{-\beta}\quad(t\ge1),
\]
uniformly in \(\varepsilon\). Its first crowding moment diverges:
on \(r\ge2\varepsilon\) it dominates a constant times
\(\int_{2\varepsilon}^1r^{-2a}dr\).
This verifies the functional distinction; it is not claimed to be
a new planar example with the dimensional hypotheses of the main theorem.

Here is a geometric sufficient condition for the fractional moment.
Rescale the source support once so its diameter is at most one. For
\(x\ne x'\), set
\[
 \theta_{x,x'}=\frac{x-x'}{|x-x'|},\qquad
 c_{x,x'}=\frac{|x|^2-|x'|^2}{2|x-x'|}.
\]
For dyadic \(0<u\le1\), put
\begin{equation}\label{crowding:richquantity}
 Q_{\varepsilon,u}(x,y)
 =\frac1{\varepsilon u^{s-1}}
  \int_{u/2<|x-x'|\le u} a_A(x',y)
   \mathbf1_{\{|y\cdot\theta_{x,x'}-c_{x,x'}|
                    \le 2b_0\varepsilon/u\}}\,d\mu(x').
\end{equation}
This counts source partners whose perpendicular bisectors pass near
the retained pin. The normalization is \(\varepsilon u^{s-1}\),
and rich pairs are measured using \(\Pi_A\), so both factors from
the same fixed positive kernel are retained.

\begin{proposition}[A rich-incidence tail criterion]
\label{crowding:richtail}
Suppose \(\beta>0\), \(\varkappa\ge0\), and \(C_*\ge1\) satisfy,
uniformly along \(\varepsilon_j\to0\), for every dyadic
\(u>\varepsilon_j^\alpha\),
\begin{equation}\label{crowding:richhyp}
 \Pi_A\{Q_{\varepsilon_j,u}>t\}
 \le C_*u^{-\varkappa}t^{-\beta}\quad(t\ge1),
 \qquad \varkappa<\beta(s-1).
\end{equation}
Then \(\Lambda_A\) has an \(L^{1+\gamma}(d\nu\,dt)\) density for
every \(0<\gamma<\min\{1,\beta\}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A distance collision with \(u/2<|x-x'|\le u\) implies
\[
 2|x-x'|\,|c_{x,x'}-y\cdot\theta_{x,x'}|
 =\big||x-y|^2-|x'-y|^2\big|\le2b_0\varepsilon.
\]
It is therefore counted in \eqref{crowding:richquantity}, giving
\[
 F_\varepsilon(x,y)
 \le\sum_{\substack{u\text{ dyadic}\le1\\u>\varepsilon^\alpha}}
           u^{s-1}Q_{\varepsilon,u}(x,y).
\]
The annulus crossing the source cutoff may count extra pairs, which
only increases this upper bound. Combining \eqref{crowding:richhyp}
with the bound one for every level-set mass, and splitting layer cake
at \((C_*u^{-\varkappa})^{1/\beta}\), gives
\[
 \int Q_{\varepsilon,u}^\gamma\,d\Pi_A
 \le\frac\beta{\beta-\gamma}
       C_*^{\gamma/\beta}u^{-\varkappa\gamma/\beta}.
\]
Subadditivity now yields
\[
 \int F_\varepsilon^\gamma\,d\Pi_A
 \le\frac\beta{\beta-\gamma}C_*^{\gamma/\beta}
       \sum_{u\text{ dyadic}\le1}
         u^{\gamma(s-1-\varkappa/\beta)}<\infty.
\]
Proposition~\ref{crowding:fractional} applies.
\end{proof}

For finite equal-mass source cells, \eqref{crowding:richquantity} is
a normalized weighted count of cells satisfying the approximate
bisector incidence. It is neither a source-only bisector mixed norm
nor a pointwise bound on pin mass for each source pair.

The missing input is \eqref{crowding:richhyp}. Angular domination alone
gives only
\[
 \int Q_{\varepsilon,u}\,d\Pi_A
 \le CA(1+u^2/\varepsilon).
\]
Indeed the bisector band implies a distance collision with a fixed
constant enlargement of its width, and the near-source angular
calculation applies at radius \(u\). If the pins are additionally
\(t\)-Frostman, \(1<t\le2\), covering the bisector strip instead gives
\[
 \int Q_{\varepsilon,u}\,d\Pi_A
 \le C(u/\varepsilon)^{2-t}.
\]
Its strip width is \(O(\varepsilon/u)\), its pin mass is at most
\(C(\varepsilon/u)^{t-1}\), and source partners have mass at most
\(Cu^s\), proving this bound. For fixed \(u>0\), these estimates
may diverge as \(\varepsilon\to0\) when \(t<2\).
Markov's inequality or fractional interpolation retains that loss.
No relation between the incidence exponents
\(\beta,\varkappa\) and Hausdorff/packing dimensions is proved here;
in particular \eqref{crowding:richhyp} is not a new dimensional theorem.

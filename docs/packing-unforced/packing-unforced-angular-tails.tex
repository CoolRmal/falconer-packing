\subsection{Using the common angular tail in the positive comparison}
\label{tails:section}

The power-law estimate for discarded pins can be replaced by the
actual common tail of their radial densities. The centers must then be
chosen simultaneously for the prescribed comparison schedule. This
improves the summability condition without changing the underlying
positive source--pin law.

Use the separated compact probabilities and radial densities
\((\Theta_x)_*\nu=\rho_x\sigma\) from
Section~\ref{sec:coherenttree}, with \(I_1(\mu)<\infty\). Define
$$
T_x(A)=\int\rho_x(\theta)1_{\{\rho_x(\theta)>A\}}\,d\sigma(\theta),
\qquad T(A)=\int T_x(A)\,d\mu(x).
$$
The densities have jointly measurable versions, obtained as the
Radon--Nikodym density of the joint source--angle law. Fix strictly
increasing sufficiently large integer depths \(n_j\), and put
\(V_j=W_{n_j,n_{j+1}}\), as in
\eqref{band:energy-coefficient}. For an \(s\)-Frostman source,
\(1<s<2\), one may instead use the larger coefficient
\(V_j=Z_{n_j,n_{j+1}}\) from Proposition~\ref{band:comparison},
absorbing a fixed constant. Both choices are independent of the centers.

\begin{proposition}[Common-tail convergence]
\label{tails:criterion}
If thresholds \(A_j\geq1\) satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{tails:series}
\sum_j\bigl[\sqrt{A_jV_j}+T(A_j)\bigr]<\infty,
\end{equation}
then \((d_y)_*\mu\) is absolutely continuous for
\(\nu\)-almost every \(y\). Equivalently, it suffices that
\begin{equation}\label{tails:optimized}
\sum_j\Phi(V_j)<\infty,
\qquad
\Phi(v)=\inf_{A\geq1}\{\sqrt{Av}+T(A)\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A center at the selected level \(n_j\) participates in only the two
adjacent comparisons. Set
$$
H_j(x)=T_x(A_{j-1})+T_x(A_j)\quad(j\geq1),
\qquad H_0(x)=T_x(A_0).
$$
In each positive-mass level-\(n_j\) cell choose, once and for all,
\(x_Q\) where the radial law is defined and
$$
H_j(x_Q)\leq \frac2{p_Q}\int_Q H_j\,d\mu.
$$
An average of zero means that almost every point has value zero;
otherwise the averaging inequality supplies admissible centers on a
positive-mass subset. All radial and projection assertions can be
imposed simultaneously over the countably many cells. Unused levels
may have arbitrary admissible centers. Thus one fixed sequence of
positive affine laws \(f_n\) is used throughout.

For a descendant \(Q\) at level \(n_{j+1}\), with ancestor \(P\)
at level \(n_j\), the pin mass discarded at threshold \(A_j\) is at
most \(T_{x_P}(A_j)+T_{x_Q}(A_j)\), directly by the angular
pushforward identity. The good term is still
\(C\sqrt{A_jV_j}\). Write
$$
S_j(A)=\sum_{Q\text{ at level }n_j}p_QT_{x_Q}(A).
$$
The bad first-norm difference is bounded by twice the discarded mass,
and descendant masses sum to their ancestor masses. Therefore
$$
\|f_{n_{j+1}}-f_{n_j}\|_1
\leq C\sqrt{A_jV_j}+2S_j(A_j)+2S_{j+1}(A_j).
$$
The simultaneous center choice controls the total rather than each
individual tail:
\begin{equation}\label{tails:centers}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_j[S_j(A_j)+S_{j+1}(A_j)]
&=\sum_j\sum_{Q\text{ at level }n_j}p_QH_j(x_Q)\\
&\leq2\sum_j\int H_j\,d\mu
=4\sum_jT(A_j).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Tonelli justifies the rearrangement. Hence \eqref{tails:series} gives
first-norm convergence of the positive affine laws. Their mass-one
limit is the actual joint distance law, as in
Theorem~\ref{band:criterion}. For \eqref{tails:optimized}, choose
thresholds within an additive \(2^{-j}\) of the respective infima.

If desired, the earlier center condition
\(F_Q\leq2p_Q^{-1}\int_Q\|\rho_x\|_q^q\,d\mu(x)\)
can also be imposed. Replace the center bound for \(H_j\) by four
times its average. Markov bounds the two excluded proportions by
\(1/2\) and \(1/4\), leaving a positive-mass intersection. Only
the absolute constant in \eqref{tails:centers} changes.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A weaker summability criterion from the same radial moment]
\label{tails:rank}
Assume
$$
B=\int\!\int\rho_x^q\,d\sigma\,d\mu<\infty,
\qquad q>1,\qquad r=q-1,\qquad\eta=\frac{r}{2r+1}.
$$
Let \(V_k^*\) be the decreasing rearrangement of the positive
coefficients \(V_j\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{tails:rank-series}
\sum_{k\geq1} k^{1/(2r)}\sqrt{V_k^*}<\infty
\end{equation}
implies almost-everywhere pinned absolute continuity. This condition
is strictly weaker than \(\sum_j V_j^\eta<\infty\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assign the threshold \(A_j=k^{1/r}\) to the index of decreasing
rank \(k\), breaking ties by the original index. Finite
\eqref{tails:rank-series} ensures that the coefficients tend to zero
and that each positive superlevel set is finite, so this enumeration
exists. The sum of good terms is exactly
\eqref{tails:rank-series}. The tail terms satisfy the exact counting
bound
\begin{equation}\label{tails:rank-count}
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k\geq1}T(k^{1/r})
&=\int\!\int\rho_x(\theta)
       \#\{k\geq1:k^{1/r}<\rho_x(\theta)\}
                      \,d\sigma(\theta)d\mu(x)\\
&\leq\int\!\int\rho_x^{1+r}\,d\sigma\,d\mu=B.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Apply Proposition~\ref{tails:criterion}. Zero coefficients, if
present in a larger version of the criterion, can receive separate
thresholds with summable tails.

For the asserted inclusion, put \(a_k=(V_k^*)^\eta\) and
\(t=1/(2\eta)=1+1/(2r)>1\). If \(S=\sum_k a_k<\infty\),
monotonicity gives \(ka_k\leq S\), and hence
$$
\sum_k k^{1/(2r)}\sqrt{V_k^*}
=\sum_k k^{t-1}a_k^t\leq S^{t-1}\sum_k a_k=S^t.
$$
The inclusion is strict: for
\(V_k^*=k^{-1/\eta}(\log k)^{-\beta}\), \(k\geq3\),
\eqref{tails:rank-series} holds when \(\beta>2\), whereas
\(\sum_k(V_k^*)^\eta<\infty\) requires
\(\beta>1/\eta>2\).
\end{proof}

For a concrete critical covering consequence, either the weighted
count or the ordinary occupied-cell count may be used in
\begin{equation}\label{tails:covering}
\mathcal N_{n+1}
\leq C2^{n(2s-1)}n^{-1/\eta}(\log n)^{-\beta},
\qquad \beta>2.
\end{equation}
The resulting coefficients are dominated by the decreasing model in
the proof, so their rearrangement satisfies
\eqref{tails:rank-series}. The earlier sum of \(V_n^\eta\)
needed \(\beta>1/\eta\) at this same critical polynomial rate.

\begin{remark}[The logarithmic boundary of this numerical test]
\label{tails:boundary}
Finite \(L^q\) data alone cannot replace \(\beta>2\) by
\(\beta=2\) uniformly within the exact threshold test
\eqref{tails:optimized}. Here is a scalar obstruction, without a
claim of geometric realizability. Fix \(0<\epsilon\leq2r\), and
choose a probability density \(\rho\) on the normalized circle with
survival function, for large \(A\),
$$
\sigma\{\rho>A\}\asymp
\frac{A^{-q}}{\log A\,(\log\log A)^{1+\epsilon}}.
$$
Such a density is obtained by the inverse distribution construction
and normalization by its finite positive first moment. Its
\(q\)-moment is finite by the tail integral
\(\int^\infty [A\log A(\log\log A)^{1+\epsilon}]^{-1}dA\).
Its density-weighted tail is
$$
T(A)\asymp
\frac{A^{-r}}{\log A\,(\log\log A)^{1+\epsilon}},
$$
since \(T(A)=A\sigma\{\rho>A\}
+\int_A^\infty\sigma\{\rho>t\}\,dt\).
For \(V_n=n^{-1/\eta}(\log n)^{-2}\), set
$$
A_n^*=n^{1/r}
       (\log\log n)^{-2(1+\epsilon)/(2r+1)}.
$$
Both \(\sqrt{A_n^*V_n}\) and \(T(A_n^*)\) are comparable to
$$
\frac1{n\log n\,(\log\log n)^{(1+\epsilon)/(2r+1)}}.
$$
For every \(A\geq A_n^*\) the good term is at least this large;
for every \(A\leq A_n^*\) the tail term is at least this large.
Its sum diverges because \((1+\epsilon)/(2r+1)\leq1\).
This proves failure of the numerical certificate for these abstract
tails and coefficients. It does not prove failure of actual affine
convergence or of positive distance length for any planar set.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A logarithmic radial moment at the boundary]
\label{tails:log-moment}
If, for some \(\kappa>0\),
$$
\int\!\int\rho_x^q[\log(e+\rho_x)]^\kappa
                         \,d\sigma\,d\mu<\infty,
$$
then the boundary coefficients
\(V_k^*\leq Ck^{-1/\eta}(\log k)^{-2}\) do satisfy
the common-tail criterion. If \(\kappa>2r\), even
\(V_k^*\leq Ck^{-1/\eta}\) suffices.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose \(a>0\) with \(ar<\kappa\) and assign the large-rank
thresholds \(A_k=k^{1/r}(\log k)^{-a}\). There are at most
$$
C\bigl[1+z^r(\log(e+z))^{ar}\bigr]
$$
ranks with \(A_k<z\). Indeed \(\log A_k\geq(\log k)/(2r)\)
eventually; this first bounds \(\log k\) by \(2r\log z\), and
substitution into \(k<z^r(\log k)^{ar}\) proves the count.
Tonelli therefore makes the total tail finite under the stated
moment. At the logarithmic boundary the good terms are bounded by
\(C/[k(\log k)^{1+a/2}]\), which is summable for every \(a>0\).
At the pure-power boundary they are
\(C/[k(\log k)^{a/2}]\); take \(2<a<\kappa/r\).
Proposition~\ref{tails:criterion} applies in both cases.
\end{proof}
